After months of tweaking, debugging, and yelling at my microphone when things broke, JARVIS has finally transitioned from a fun side project into an indispensable part of my daily workflow.

## Current Capabilities

Right now, JARVIS runs locally on my workstation and seamlessly manages several aspects of my day:

- **Coding Assistant:** JARVIS acts as my pair programmer. I can highlight code, ask JARVIS to optimize it, and it writes directly to my IDE via a custom extension I built.
- **Environment Management:** With a simple voice command, JARVIS can spin up Docker containers, configure environment variables, and launch all the necessary dev servers for my specific projects.
- **Email & Schedule:** It summarizes my morning emails and reads out my schedule. If I tell it I'm running late, it drafts emails to the respective meeting participants for my approval.
- **Smart Home Integration:** Through Home Assistant APIs, JARVIS controls the lighting in my room based on the time of day and whether I am in "focus mode" for coding.

## The Future of JARVIS

While I am incredibly proud of where JARVIS is today, there is always room for improvement. The next major milestone is **Proactive Autonomy**.

Currently, JARVIS is largely reactive—it waits for my commands. I am working on background agents that monitor system health, watch for server errors in my deployed apps, and notify me proactively. I also plan to experiment with multimodal inputs, allowing JARVIS to truly "see" my screen continuously and understand visual context without me having to explicitly screenshot it.

The journey of building JARVIS has been a masterclass in LLM engineering, and I can't wait to see how it evolves as AI technology continues to advance.
